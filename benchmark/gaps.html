▶ How to read this report — click to collapse

This flips the comparison matrix into a decision. Pick your wallet and a set of rivals; the page auto-derives the two lists a PM actually needs:

Gaps

Every metric where at least one rival is better for the user than you, sorted by how many rivals beat you. The big number on the left is "beaten by k of n rivals" — the top of the list is your most-contested gap. This is your what-to-build-next backlog.

Strengths

Metrics where you beat every selected rival — your defensible positioning. (A tie doesn't count as a win.)

How "better" is decided

  • Cells are ranked by the same better/worse-for-the-user polarity as the matrix: Good > Partial > Worse. Counts compare numerically (more audits = better, fewer open high-severity flaws = better).
  • — Unknown cells are not ranked — a missing data point is never counted as a win or a loss for either side.
  • Descriptive Value metrics (e.g. licence, default transfer mode) have no better/worse order, so they're excluded from win/lose.
  • Click any row for the evidence: where you and each rival stand, with the source citations.

Use Platform to compare a fair peer set, and Copy backlog to paste the gap list straight into a planning doc.
