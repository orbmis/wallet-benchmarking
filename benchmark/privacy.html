This is a Sankey diagram. It traces your personal data as it leaves a wallet: it flows left → right through three columns, and the thickness of each ribbon shows how much data moves along that path.

What the colours mean

Ribbon colour = collection policy
  • Always — sent every time; the user cannot turn it off.
  • By default — on unless the user disables it.
  • Opt-in — only if the user explicitly enables it (least invasive).
Recipient colour = privacy risk
  • Data broker / exchange — highest concern.
  • Chain / off-chain / corporate infrastructure.
  • Wallet developer / auditor — lowest concern.

Risk comes from each company's classification in the dataset. A ⚠ on a recipient means we couldn't match it to a known company, so it has no risk rating.

How to use it

  • Pick a wallet from the cards below — they're sorted worst-first, and the red badge counts data brokers/exchanges.
  • Hover any ribbon for the exact data types, policy, and the stated purpose of collection.
  • Click any node (a coloured bar) to isolate just its connected flows and grey out the rest. Click it again to reset.
  • Filter with the controls below: hide policies (e.g. show only Always flows that can't be disabled), or focus on brokers & exchanges only.
  • For wallets with platform-specific behaviour (e.g. Rabby), a Platform selector appears.

Only wallets whose data flows have been mapped in the dataset appear here — currently 5 of 32. An empty or sparse diagram means less was recorded, not necessarily that the wallet is more private.
